A continent of extraordinary habitats

One region. Many worlds of wildlife.

India's national parks and tiger reserves protect an extraordinary range of landscapes: sal forests, grasslands, wetlands, mangroves, tropical forests and high Himalayan terrain. Each has its own character, wildlife and way of exploring.

Beyond India, Nepal's Chitwan and Bardia and Sri Lanka's Yala, Wilpattu and Udawalawe offer compelling extensions for travellers drawn to wild places. The best itinerary is shaped around the species you hope to encounter, the season and the style of safari you prefer.

A signature Indian safari

Ranthambore. Where the forest tells its own story.

Ranthambore brings together dry forest, rocky ridges, lakes and historic architecture in one of India's most recognisable tiger landscapes. A thoughtfully paced stay can include guided safari drives, time in the surrounding region and carefully selected accommodation.

Tiger sightings are never guaranteed. We encourage patient, respectful wildlife viewing and follow park regulations and the instructions of authorised naturalists and guides.

Beyond the classic national parks

Leopard country. Two very different worlds.

Some of India's most compelling wildlife journeys take place in conservation reserves and distinctive landscapes beyond the traditional national-park circuit. Jawai and Jhalana deserve their own place in a considered Rajasthan itinerary.

Pali district · Rajasthan · Leopard landscape

Jawai – the granite hills.

Jawai's boulder-strewn hills, scrub and reservoirs create a striking landscape where leopards share space with local communities. It is a conservation-reserve landscape, not a national park, and is suited to a slower, place-led leopard journey.

Leopard · VulnerableRocky scrub habitat

Rajasthan protected-area records ↗
Jaipur · Rajasthan · Urban forest

Jhalana–Amagarh – wilderness beside the city.

Set within Jaipur's Aravalli landscape, Jhalana is known for leopard habitat in a compact dry-deciduous forest. Nearby Amagarh adds another conservation landscape to consider. It pairs naturally with Jaipur's forts, palaces and heritage stays.

Leopard · VulnerableAravalli dry forest

Official Jhalana brochure ↗

Jawai and Jhalana are presented as conservation / leopard landscapes, not mislabelled as national parks. Sightings remain subject to wildlife movement, local protocols and authorised safari operations.

Beyond India · Regional safari journeys

Continue into Nepal & Sri Lanka.

For travellers with more time, a wildlife itinerary can extend beyond India. Nepal and Sri Lanka each bring a distinct natural setting and a different rhythm of discovery.

Nepal · Terai wilderness

Rhino country & riverine forests.

Nepal's lowland national parks combine grasslands, river systems and subtropical forests. Chitwan is known for one-horned rhinoceros, gharial and rich birdlife; Bardia offers a more remote-feeling western Terai wilderness.

Chitwan National Park ↗ Bardiya / Bardia listings ↗

Explore jeep drives, canoe journeys, guided nature walks where permitted and opportunities to learn about Tharu culture. Activities are subject to park rules and local conditions.

Sri Lanka · Island wildlife

Leopards, elephants & wild landscapes.

Sri Lanka offers varied wildlife experiences within a compact island. Yala is renowned for leopard habitat; Wilpattu is known for forest and natural lakes; Udawalawe is strongly associated with elephant country.

Travel with consideration

The wild deserves respect.

The quality of a wildlife journey is measured not only by what is seen, but by how the encounter takes place. We encourage low-impact choices and adherence to the rules established by each protected area.

Keep distanceNever pressure, approach or surround wild animals for photographs.
Follow park rulesRespect routes, vehicle limits, guide instructions and restricted areas.
Choose responsiblyPrioritise authorised operators and wildlife experiences that avoid animal exploitation.
Stay patientWildlife is unpredictable. Quiet observation is part of the experience.
